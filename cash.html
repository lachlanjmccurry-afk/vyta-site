Product Cash

Where has the cash gone?

It’s the first question every owner asks. Vyta answers it from the books, with one chart that walks from the bank balance a year ago to the balance today.

Reading the waterfall

Six steps from opening to closing cash

Each bar adds to or takes away from the opening balance. Green bars brought cash in; red bars took it out.

TEST – Stressed Co, Sep 2025 to Sep 2026

A made-up business. Cash went from $8,680 to an overdraft of $30,000.

Operating profit

Net profit from the profit and loss report. Here, a loss of $100,537 drained the account.

ATO movement

A rising ATO balance keeps cash in the business for now, but it’s a debt that must be paid. Here it grew by $56,674.

Debt movement

New bank loans bring cash in; repayments take it out. Overdrafts are already inside the cash figure.

Change in debtors

When customers owe more, sales have been made but the cash hasn’t arrived. Customers here owe $34,081 more.

Change in creditors

When the business owes suppliers more, it has held on to cash by paying later. Suppliers here are owed $129,102 more.

Other

Everything else, such as owner drawings, buying or selling equipment, and non-cash costs like depreciation.

Month by month

The bank balance at every month-end

Under the waterfall, a line shows the cash balance at the end of each month, so you can see when the slide started, not just where it ended.

In this made-up example, cash went below zero in January 2026 and kept falling by about $3,500 a month.
