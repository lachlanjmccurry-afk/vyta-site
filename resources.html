Glossary

The terms that matter, in plain English

Insolvent
Unable to pay all your debts as and when they fall due. It’s a cash test: a business can own a lot and still be insolvent if it can’t pay its bills on time.
Current ratio
Short-term assets divided by short-term debts. Below 1 means the debts due within a year are bigger than what can be turned into cash in that time.
Cash runway
How many months the cash would last if it kept going out at its recent rate.
Creditor days
On average, how many days suppliers wait to be paid. Rising creditor days often means a business is using its suppliers as a bank.
Debtor days
On average, how many days customers take to pay. When it rises, sales are being made but the cash is arriving later.
Gross margin
What’s left from each dollar of sales after the direct cost of making that sale, before rent, wages and other overheads.
Underlying profit
Profit before interest, depreciation and one-off costs. It shows whether the core business makes money once the noise is stripped out.
Equity ratio
What the owners’ stake (net assets) is as a share of everything the business owns. Below zero means the business owes more than it owns.
Statutory demand
A formal written demand for a debt of at least $4,000. If it isn’t paid or challenged within 21 days, the company is presumed insolvent and the creditor can apply to wind it up.
Director penalty notice (DPN)
A notice from the ATO that can make directors personally liable for the company’s unpaid PAYG withholding, GST and superannuation guarantee charge.
Safe harbour
A protection for directors from personal liability for insolvent trading while they develop a plan that’s reasonably likely to lead to a better outcome than administration or liquidation.
Small business restructuring (SBR)
A formal process for eligible small companies, with debts under $1 million, to agree a plan with creditors while the directors stay in control.
Voluntary administration (VA)
An independent administrator takes control of the company and creditors vote on its future, often a deed of company arrangement.
Creditors’ voluntary liquidation (CVL)
The directors and shareholders start the process of closing an insolvent company, and a registered liquidator sells its assets and pays creditors as far as possible.

These guides are general information only and aren’t financial, legal, tax or insolvency advice. Laws and ATO practices change. Speak to a qualified adviser about your situation.
