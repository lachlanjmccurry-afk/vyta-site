Viability indicators

If the cash problem were fixed, would the business make money? Higher is better.

Viability indicators
IDIndicator and what it checksWarning levelsWeight
V1Equity ratioThe owners' stake (net assets) as a share of everything the business owns.Amber 0.20 or belowRed 0.00 or below15
V2Gross marginWhat is left from each dollar of sales after the direct cost of making the sale.Amber 30% or belowRed 15% or below15
V3Margin trendWhether gross margin is improving or slipping compared with the year before. Needs 24 months of data.Amber −2.0 pts or belowRed −5.0 pts or below10

Warning levels are Vyta’s working settings and are reviewed as the product is tested with real businesses.

Where they come from

Built on the signs Australian courts look for.

In ASIC v Plymin (2003), the Supreme Court of Victoria set out a checklist of signs that a company may be insolvent. Courts and practitioners still use it today. Vyta turns as many of those signs as possible into measurements it can take from Xero every day.

Read the guide to the warning signs
  • Continuing losses become underlying margin and overheads against gross profit (V4, V5).
  • Liquidity ratios below one become the current and quick ratios (D1, D2).
  • Overdue taxes become ATO debt rising month after month (D4).
  • Creditors unpaid outside trading terms become creditor days and bills 90+ days overdue (D7, D8).
  • Pressure from the bank becomes bank debt growth and overdraft use (D5, D6).
