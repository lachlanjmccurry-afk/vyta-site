Product Distress & Viability

Two scores, because trouble and worth are different questions.

A business can be in deep trouble and still be worth saving. Another can look calm while its model is quietly broken. Vyta scores them separately, then puts them together.

Scored 0 to 100Refreshed dailyEvery figure shown
The two scores

What each score asks

Distress · higher is worse

How much financial trouble is the business in right now?

Built from 13 indicators drawn from the court-tested signs of insolvency: whether short-term debts can be paid, how long the cash lasts, whether ATO and bank debts are growing, and how long suppliers and customers are waiting.

0 Low30 Moderate60 High
Viability · higher is better

If the cash problem were fixed, would the business make money?

Built from 8 indicators about the business underneath: gross margin and its trend, overheads against gross profit, underlying profit, revenue trend, and what it owns against what it owes.

0 Weak40 Uncertain65 Strong

Under the hood

How a score is worked out

Each indicator is rated Green, Amber or Red against its warning levels. Each rating is worth points, and each indicator has a weight that reflects how much it matters. The score is the weighted average, from 0 to 100.

Indicators Vyta can’t assess, because the data isn’t in Xero or the check needs an answer from the owner, are left out of the average rather than counted as zero.

Example: the heaviest-weighted viability indicator is underlying profit margin (weight 20). The heaviest distress indicator is ATO debt rising (weight 12).

Data confidence

Every result says how much data it rests on: High with 12 months of trading, Medium with 6 to 11, and Low under 6. With little data, treat the scores as an early indication.

Owner questions

Some things can’t be read from Xero, like whether the bank has extended limits or what the pipeline looks like. Vyta lists ten questions only the owner can answer, and what each one feeds into.

Statutory demand override

An unpaid statutory demand is a formal insolvency trigger. If the business has had one in the last six months, Distress goes straight to High, whatever the other indicators say.
